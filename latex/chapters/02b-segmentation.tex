\section{Segmentation and Correspondence}
\label{sec:correspondence-segmentation}

The pipeline rests on two operations that the representations of
\cref{sec:visual-representation} do not by themselves provide. The first is
segmentation, delineating the pixels that belong to a given object in a single frame.
The second is cross-view object correspondence, which is segmentation enriched with
information from a second camera; given an object isolated in one view, the task is to
recover the same object in another view, and that demands first establishing how the
two views relate. We therefore treat segmentation as the primitive operation and
correspondence as the capability built upon it, and in doing so fix the mask and
matching symbols that recur throughout the chapters that follow.

\subsection{Segmentation and the Segment Anything Models}
\label{subsec:sam}

Segmentation asks which pixels constitute an object. The modern lineage begins with
the fully convolutional network of Long et al.\ in 2015 \cite{long2015fcn}, which
assigns a class to every pixel in one forward pass; the \textit{U-Net} of Ronneberger
et al.\ \cite{ronneberger2015unet}, whose encoder-decoder with skip connections
recovers the fine spatial detail lost to downsampling; and \textit{Mask R-CNN}
\cite{he2017maskrcnn}, which first proposes candidate regions and then segments within
each, separating one instance of a class from another. Sharper boundaries followed from
combining atrous convolution with structured post-processing in DeepLab
\cite{chen2017deeplabsemanticimagesegmentation}. Across these works the task is read in
several ways, labelling every pixel with a class, isolating each object instance, or
doing both at once, and a further line lets a user guide the result by clicking on the
target, refined iteratively in RITM \cite{sofiiuk2021revivingiterativetrainingmask}.
The accumulation of task-specific heads motivated a single promptable model able to
segment anything asked of it.

That model is the \textit{Segment Anything Model (SAM)}, presented by Kirillov et al.\
in 2023 \cite{kirillov2023sam}, and it is built from three components. An image encoder,
a Vision Transformer pre-trained as the masked autoencoder of \cref{subsec:dino}, runs
once per image to produce a dense feature grid. A prompt encoder maps a user signal, a
point, a box, or a coarse mask, into a small set of tokens using the positional and
learned embeddings of \cref{subsec:vit}. A lightweight mask decoder, a Transformer that
lets image features and prompt tokens attend to one another in both directions, then
turns the two into an output,

\begin{equation}
  (\mask, s) = D\big(\,E(\img),\, P(\text{prompt})\,\big),
  \label{eq:sam-decoder}
\end{equation}

where $E$ is the image encoder, $P$ the prompt encoder, $D$ the decoder, $\mask$ the
predicted binary segmentation mask, and $s$ the decoder's own estimate of the mask's
quality. That estimate is an intersection-over-union score, $\iou(\mask, \mask') =
\abs{\mask \cap \mask'} / \abs{\mask \cup \mask'}$, the overlap between a predicted mask
$\mask$ and a reference $\mask'$ measured as the ratio of their intersection to their
union, which the model predicts as $s$ without seeing the reference. Because a single
prompt is often ambiguous, where a point on a shirt may mean the shirt or the whole
person, the decoder emits three candidate masks and the predicted score $s$ selects
among them. Separating the heavy image encoder, which runs once, from the cheap prompt
encoder and decoder, which run for each prompt, is what makes the model interactive, and
training it on the very large \textit{SA-1B} collection of masks gives it the generality
to segment objects it was never explicitly taught.

SAM operates on still images, yet our pipeline must follow an object through a video,
and SAM 2 \cite{ravi2024sam2} extends the design to that setting without re-encoding
the whole clip at every step. It keeps the per-frame encoder of \cref{eq:sam-decoder}
and adds a streaming memory. A memory encoder writes selected past frames and their
predicted masks into a memory bank $\set{M}$, and a memory attention module lets the
current frame attend to $\set{M}$ through the cross-attention of
\cref{subsec:transformer}, so that the prediction at frame $\frameidx$ is conditioned on
what the model has already segmented rather than computed afresh. A separate occlusion
head outputs whether the object is present at all, which stops the decoder from
inventing a mask when the target has left the view, a frequent event under the heavy
occlusion of egocentric footage. This memory architecture is the part of SAM 2 that the
pipeline relies on most heavily, and we return to it in detail when we describe
bidirectional propagation in \cref{sec:addition-of-time}. The promptable design of
\cref{eq:sam-decoder} is also the foundation that \cref{sec:language-grounding} builds
on, where a noun phrase replaces the point or box as the prompt.

\subsection{Feature Matching and Dense Correspondence}
\label{subsec:matching}

Carrying a mask from one camera to another is segmentation given a second view, and it
turns on a prior question, namely which point in one image corresponds to which point in
the other. Correspondence was first approached through handcrafted local descriptors.
The \textit{SIFT} descriptor of Lowe in 2004 \cite{lowe2004sift}, followed by
\textit{SURF} \cite{bay2008surf} and \textit{ORB} \cite{rublee2011orb}, detects a sparse
set of keypoints and encodes the neighbourhood of each into a vector built to be
invariant to scale and rotation; two images are then matched by pairing keypoints whose
descriptors are nearest in this space. These methods remain remarkably durable, but
their invariances are designed rather than learned, and they degrade sharply under the
wide changes in viewpoint and illumination that separate an egocentric from an
exocentric camera.

Learning replaced design in two stages. SuperPoint \cite{detone2018superpoint} trains a
single network to detect keypoints and emit descriptors jointly, and SuperGlue
\cite{sarlin2020superglue} matches two such sets with an attention-based graph network
that reasons about all candidate pairs at once rather than matching each keypoint in
isolation. LoFTR \cite{sun2021loftr} then dispensed with detection altogether, applying
the self-attention and cross-attention of \cref{subsec:transformer} between two images to
produce dense matches directly, so that correspondences can be recovered even in the
textureless regions where keypoint detectors find nothing to anchor to.

RoMa, introduced by Edstedt et al.\ in 2024 \cite{roma}, is the model the pipeline
adopts, and it carries the dense paradigm to its robust extreme. Rather than a list of
matched points, RoMa predicts a dense \textit{warp field} $\warp$ together with a
per-coordinate confidence,

\begin{equation}
  (\warp, c) = f_\theta(\img_a, \img_b),
  \qquad
  \warp(\vv{x}) \in \R^2,
  \quad
  c(\vv{x}) \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}

where $f_\theta$ is the matcher applied to a source image $\img_a$ and a target image
$\img_b$. For every coordinate $\vv{x}$ in the source, the warp $\warp(\vv{x})$ returns
the coordinate in the target that it judges to correspond, so that $\warp$ is a complete
deformation carrying one image onto the other, and the confidence $c(\vv{x})$ reports how
reliable that single match is, near one where the geometry is certain and near zero where
the source point is occluded or has no counterpart. RoMa estimates the field coarsely
from the self-supervised features of \cref{subsec:dino}, which give it a semantic anchor
that survives wide changes in viewpoint, and then refines it locally to pixel precision.
The confidence is what makes the warp field usable as evidence rather than a raw
prediction; the pipeline ranks candidate frames by the number of confident matches whose
source coordinate falls inside the object mask $\mask$, turning \cref{eq:warp} into a
language-free signal for selecting where to look. This same property of matching by
semantic similarity is what allows the patch features of \cref{subsec:dino} to put two
images into correspondence without any geometric prior, of which the egocentric to
exocentric setting is the most demanding instance.
