\chapter{Theory}
\label{chap:theory}
To justify our pipeline in \cref{chap:pipeline}, we need to first study the foundation models behind it. This chapter builds from the theoretical foundations to the most advanced topics. We open with visual representation (\cref{sec:visual-representation}), moving from the Transformer to the Vision Transformer and finally to self-supervised feature learning. Segmentation (\cref{sec:segmentation}) then builds on these representations, reviewing prior works and concluding with the Segment Anything Model and its extension to video. Yet a segmenter alone cannot decide which object to extract across two unrelated viewpoints. Therefore, we present language grounding (\cref{sec:language-grounding}), which supplies the bridge, because a description is invariant to perspective. We detail how vision and language are aligned, and then how Grounding DINO and SAM~3 turn a phrase into a mask. Orchestrating the models into an autonomous system is not trivial. Hence, we resort to large vision language models (\cref{sec:foundation-models-agents}) and how they can be embedded in reasoning loops that allows them to act with tools. We close by surveying related work on cross-view object correspondence (\cref{sec:related-works}), and identify the common limitation that our pipeline addresses.
